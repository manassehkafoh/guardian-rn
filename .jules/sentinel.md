## 2024-06-20 - Insecure Constant-Time Equal Functions
**Vulnerability:** The constant-time comparison functions for HMAC verification in TypeScript, Kotlin, and Swift contained two critical flaws: 1) They returned early if the lengths of the two strings didn't match, allowing for timing attacks to deduce the expected HMAC length. 2) The iteration loops were bound by the length of the *untrusted* (user-provided) string, potentially leading to CPU exhaustion (Denial of Service) if an excessively large string was provided.
**Learning:** Even when using bitwise XOR for character comparison, an early length check completely undermines the constant-time guarantee. Additionally, iterating based on the untrusted string's length is dangerous, as attackers control this variable.
**Prevention:** Always incorporate the length check into the constant-time operation itself (e.g., XORing the lengths and incorporating that into the `diff` accumulator). Crucially, the loop must *always* iterate based on the length of the *trusted* (locally computed/expected) string, padding out-of-bounds reads from the untrusted string with zeros. In Swift, use `utf8.count` and accumulate with `Int` to avoid grapheme/byte count mismatches and signature malleability.
